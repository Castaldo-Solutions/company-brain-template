# Writing a proposal

> A worked skeleton, kept deliberately generic. Rewrite it with what you actually do: the value of this file comes from being true, not from being complete.

## When it applies

After [the qualifying step: a discovery call, an assessment, a site visit]. Never before: a proposal written without that step is a price list with a cover page.

## What to load before writing

- `company/pricing.md` for the commercial terms (confidential)
- `company/icp.md` to check this is a client you want
- `company/proof.md` for what you may cite, and about whom
- `examples/` for a proposal that worked in a similar situation

## The steps

1. **Restate the problem in the client's words.** If you cannot, you did not understand it and the proposal will not land. Their words, from the call, not your category names.
2. **State the outcome**, with a number and a timeframe. What changes for them, not what you will do.
3. **Scope**: what is in, and explicitly what is out. The second list prevents more disputes than the first.
4. **Method and phases**, with what the client sees at the end of each one.
5. **Price**, built as described in `company/pricing.md`.
6. **Proof**: one case, only if `company/proof.md` says you may cite it.
7. **Next step**, with a date. A proposal without a scheduled next step is a document that dies in an inbox.

## Before it goes out

- [ ] The outcome is measurable and I know how it will be measured.
- [ ] Nothing is promised that `company/offering.md` says we do not do.
- [ ] No client is named without recorded permission.
- [ ] Every number has a source.
- [ ] Price and scope were approved by [who decides]. An agent drafts, a person signs.

## What goes wrong

- **The proposal answers a different problem from the one they have.** Cause: you skipped step 1 and started from your template.
- **The price gets discussed before the outcome is understood.** Cause: the outcome was vague, so the only comparable thing left was the number.
- **It goes quiet.** Cause: no next step with a date, or it was sent to someone who does not decide.
